I don't always bake everything from scratch, but when I do it usually involves sourdough. Mostly, this involves boules, focaccia, pizza, etc -- but occasionally I feel like pie. Given how infrequently I make any kind of pastries, I don't usually have much by way of pastry ingredients on hand, so this leads to a few basic restrictions/requirements for a pie crust recipe:

1. Obviously, it should use sourdough starter
1. It can only use ingredients I typically have on hand -- no fancy sugars, shortening, or other stuff I would need to get especially for this
1. All ingredients should be measured by mass and Baker's percentage like I am used to with breadmaking


I found a good place to start with [Sally McKenny's](https://sallysbakingaddiction.com/all-butter-pie-crust/) recipes, especially this one using all butter. Sally does note that a mix of butter and shortening improves the recipe, but I rarely have shortening... though at some point I should try this with lard. I adjusted the recipe to replace some of the flour and water with sourdough starter, and rounded ingredient masses for easier measuring (I usually use an old analog scale I found in a thrift store since I hate changing batteries), and measured masses for the salt and sugar which the original recipe omitted.

A few more tweaks to make it more friendly to work the sourdough starter into the recipe, and this is what I came up with. The first thing I made with it was a quiche to use up some eggs I was concerned would turn before I could get to them, with a blind bake weighing the crust with beans. This proved the recipe worked, and left me with a second portion that I kept vac-packed in my freezer until I decided to make [Toll House Walnut Pie](INSERT LINK), when I also got a second chance to make more crusts and refine the process a bit.
